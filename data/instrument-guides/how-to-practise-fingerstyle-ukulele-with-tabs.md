A fingerstyle ukulele tab may combine melody and accompaniment on the same four strings. Learning every fret number in order is only part of the task. Listen for the melody, understand which notes sound together and practise how the phrase connects across the bar line.

Use the [ukulele tab editor](/ukulele-tab-editor) to keep the arrangement editable while practising. If you are starting from a performance you heard, the [ukulele melody transcription guide](/blog/how-to-transcribe-a-ukulele-melody) explains how to build the part.

## Confirm high G or low G

Check the tuning before playing the first phrase. Standard ukulele tab has A, E, C and G lines from top to bottom. With high-G tuning, that fourth string sounds G4, above the C and E strings. With low G, it sounds G3 beneath them.

A phrase that uses the fourth string may therefore change register when played on the other tuning. Choose the preset that matches your instrument and listen to those notes before rehearsing the full arrangement.

## Find the melody inside the tab

Play or sing the melody without the supporting notes. Mark its rhythm in your mind and identify where it moves across strings. This makes it easier to preserve the line once the accompaniment returns.

Not every simultaneous note has the same musical role. A held chord can support a changing melody, and a short accompaniment note can sit between two melody attacks. Follow the musical layers rather than treating the whole tab as a sequence of equal events.

## Practise a manageable phrase

Select one or two bars containing a specific difficulty: a string crossing, a chord change or a melody note that needs to continue while another finger moves. Play at a comfortable speed and keep the count steady.

Use slower practice playback rather than rewriting the piece with longer note values. Once the movement is controlled, increase the rehearsal speed and check the phrase again in context. Speed without a clear rhythm can make a fingering problem harder to diagnose.

## Check simultaneous notes and releases

Play notes together when their attacks align in the arrangement. Then listen for where each note should end. Holding everything can blur the melody, while releasing every note too soon can remove the accompaniment's support.

Choose fret positions that allow the important notes to ring when the phrase calls for it. If a fingering makes that impossible, try another position for the same pitch before changing the musical idea.

## Include the next downbeat

Practise the end of a phrase with the beginning of the next one. A shift may feel comfortable until you need to reach the following note. Including the landing helps you choose a fingering that works for the complete transition.

When looping a section, check that the return to the beginning follows the intended count. A short pause at the loop boundary can hide a transition that still needs attention.

## Keep a useful practice version

Save the arrangement when you have a version you can return to. If you simplify an accompaniment, keep the melody and rhythm intact. A native project file retains the tuning and editable part; a text tab is useful for reading the fret positions away from the editor.
