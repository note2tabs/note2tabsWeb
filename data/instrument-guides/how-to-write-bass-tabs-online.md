A bass tab becomes useful when its fret positions and rhythm describe a line you can play. Start with a short groove, choose positions that connect comfortably and listen to the rests as carefully as the notes. You can build a longer arrangement once the first phrase works.

Open the [bass tab editor](/bass-tab-editor) or start a [blank bass track](/gte/new?track=bass). If you are working from an audio performance, begin with [bass transcription](/audio-to-bass-tabs).

## Choose the tuning before entering notes

A standard four-string bass is tuned E1–A1–D2–G2 from lowest to highest. On the tab, the lines run G, D, A and E from top to bottom. A zero means the open string, while a number identifies a fret on that string.

Choose Drop D if the arrangement uses a low D string. A fret number only has the intended pitch when the tuning matches the instrument. Check the register with playback before writing a whole section.

## Write one recognisable groove

Begin with a phrase you can sing, play or count. Enter its main attacks before adding a pickup or fill. A root note repeated in a clear rhythm is enough to establish the shape of a part.

For a simple example, write open E, G at the third fret of the E string, and A at the fifth fret. Then compare that A with the open A string. The pitch is the same, but the fingering and the transition into the following note may feel different.

## Give note endings enough attention

The space after a bass note contributes to the groove. A short attack followed by silence is different from a note held into the next beat. Set note lengths deliberately rather than making every event reach the following attack.

Count the subdivision while listening back. If a note leads into a chord change just before the beat, moving it onto the beat changes the phrase. Check the musical intent before making the page appear more regular.

## Choose fingering for the whole phrase

Look at the notes before and after a difficult position. Staying on one string can make a melodic line clear, but an unnecessary large shift may be harder than using another string. Test the phrase slowly on the instrument.

Keep repeated figures consistent when they play the same role. If two versions use different fingerings, make sure there is a reason: the next section may begin in another position, or one phrase may need an open string for a shift.

## Hear the bass in the arrangement

If you are writing with drums, listen to where the bass and kick meet. Their relationship can support the groove even when they leave space for each other. Add guitar or piano parts when you need to check the harmony.

Play across the bar line before repeating the section. A pickup into the next chord belongs to the transition, not just the bar where it happens to be written.

## Save a part you can return to

Keep the project in your library or export a native file for another editing session. A text tab is useful for reading string and fret positions. MIDI carries pitches and timing into a music application.
