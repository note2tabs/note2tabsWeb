A useful YouTube piano transcription starts with a clearly chosen passage. An intro, a repeated accompaniment or a short solo phrase is easier to check than a whole performance at once. Once the notes are generated, use notation and playback to turn that section into a part you can practise.

Open the [piano transcriber in YouTube mode](/transcribe?instrument=piano&mode=youtube) with a public recording you are entitled to use. You can also upload your own audio through the [piano transcription page](/piano-transcription).

## Choose the performance you want to learn

Find a recording where you can hear the piano part clearly. A solo performance makes melody and accompaniment easy to follow. In a band recording, listen for a section where the piano phrase is distinct enough to compare with the result.

Different covers may use a different key, introduction or accompaniment. Decide which performance you are learning before searching for a particular note. A transcription of one version does not describe every arrangement of the song.

## Set the start and end of the passage

Paste the public YouTube URL, then select the section in the transcriber. The passage can begin later in the video; it does not have to be taken from the opening. The duration available depends on the account and plan shown in the interface.

Choose boundaries that leave the musical context intact. If a melody has a pickup before the downbeat, include that pickup. If you are working out a fill, include its landing in the following bar so you can hear how the phrase resolves.

## Listen once before opening the score

Count the pulse and identify the melody's first attack. Does it begin with the accompaniment or slightly before it? Does the bass hold through the whole bar, or change with each chord?

Those observations help you review the generated notation. Without them, it is easy to make a bar look neat while accidentally changing the phrasing you wanted to learn.

## Review the piano part in layers

Open the pitched piano track in the editor. Follow the melody first, then listen to the bass and inner chord tones. Check one phrase before extending the arrangement to the next section.

Use the piano roll when two notes overlap unexpectedly or you need to inspect the duration of a held chord. Use sheet view when you want to read the phrase at the keyboard. The [piano-roll and notation comparison](/blog/piano-roll-vs-sheet-music-notation) explains what each view makes easier to see.

## Practise the transition, not just the notes

Loop the section and use a comfortable playback speed. Learn a manageable part with one hand, then add the other. Include a little of the following phrase when rehearsing a difficult ending so the transition becomes part of the practice.

Return to the performance's speed and listen again before making another edit. Some differences become clearer at full speed; others need a slower pass to separate the notes.

## Keep a reusable arrangement

Save the project to return to the passage. Export MIDI for a DAW, MusicXML for a notation application or a native project file to keep working in Note2Tabs. Continue with another section only after the first one is understandable.
