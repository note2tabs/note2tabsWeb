A drum arrangement can begin with one bar. Establish the pulse, add a pattern that supports the song and decide where the groove needs a change. A fill works best when it connects two sections rather than simply adding more hits.

Open the [drum notation editor](/drum-notation-editor) or start a [blank drum track](/gte/new?track=drums) to build the pattern as you read.

## Write the pulse first

Choose the time signature and a tempo you can count comfortably. Start with a steady hi-hat or ride pattern. In 4/4, eighth notes give you a clear subdivision against which to place the rest of the kit.

Play the bar back before adding another layer. You should be able to count where every main beat falls. If the pattern feels unclear on its own, a busier kick or snare part will not make the pulse easier to follow.

## Add kick and snare with a purpose

Try kick on beats one and three and snare on two and four as a starting example. Then change one event at a time. Add a kick between the main beats, leave a space, or move an accent to hear how the groove changes.

Listen to the bass part if the project includes one. Kick and bass can reinforce each other, but they do not need identical rhythms. Decide where their attacks should meet and where one instrument should leave room for the other.

## Check what happens at the same time

Several kit pieces can sound together. A snare beneath a hi-hat attack is still two separate events, even when the notation groups them at one time position. Keep the voices distinct so you can edit one without moving the other.

Play the groove as a whole after checking the individual lanes. A pattern that looks evenly spaced can still have a different feel from the phrase you intended. Let listening guide the next edit.

## Build a fill from the existing count

Keep the subdivision running and replace a small part of the groove with a fill. Start with the last beat of a bar rather than a long sequence across the whole measure. Move through snare or tom voices while maintaining the beat count.

Include the first beat after the fill in playback. The transition may need a crash and kick together, a quieter landing or a deliberate rest. The fill should lead somewhere, not leave the next section feeling accidental.

## Repeat the pattern before adding variations

Play the groove for several repetitions. Check whether the last beat connects naturally to the first beat of the loop. A small error at that boundary can be easy to miss when listening to one bar only.

Once the basic pattern works, arrange a variation for another section. Keep the musical reason clear: a chorus might need more cymbal activity, while a verse might benefit from space. More notes are not automatically a stronger arrangement.

## Keep the part ready for practice and production

Use a slower practice playback speed to learn a difficult transition without rewriting the score. Save the project when the pattern is useful, then export MIDI if you want to continue arranging with a kit in a DAW.
