A bassline connects harmony with rhythm. A useful transcription needs the right notes, but also the rests, lengths and placement that make the groove work. Check those elements before spending time choosing a fret for every pitch.

Start with the [audio-to-bass-tabs workflow](/audio-to-bass-tabs). If you already know the part, open the [bass tab editor](/bass-tab-editor) and write it directly.

## Choose a passage you can hear clearly

Use a direct bass recording, a bass cover or a stem when you have one. For a full-band recording, choose a passage where you can follow the bassline rather than judging only the loudest low-frequency sound.

Work with one repeating groove or song section first. Listen for the point where the pattern starts again. That gives you a phrase to compare after generating the transcription.

## Check rhythm before choosing positions

Listen to the bass attacks against the beat. A note just before the snare can create forward motion; moving it onto the beat changes the line even when its pitch stays the same. Count the subdivision and check one bar at a time.

Note lengths matter too. A short note followed by a rest is different from a sustained note with the same attack time. Listen for where the bass stops, especially before a new section or a kick-and-bass accent.

## Confirm the register

Bass tab shows string and fret positions, while MIDI represents sounding pitch. Standard four-string bass tuning is E1–A1–D2–G2 from lowest to highest. Check that a low E sounds in the bass register rather than an octave above it.

Listen to the line's shape as well as individual notes. A repeated root, a climb toward the next chord or a short descending fill provides context when two possible pitches seem plausible. Replay the phrase against the original recording before changing a whole passage.

## Choose a playable fingering

The same pitch can often be played on more than one string. Pick positions that make the complete phrase comfortable, not just each note in isolation. An open string can give the hand time to shift; a fretted version may offer a more consistent articulation with the next note.

In the bass editor, start from the four-string staff and adjust string and fret choices while keeping the intended pitches. Standard E–A–D–G and Drop D serve different arrangements, so choose the tuning that matches the instrument you are playing.

## Practise the groove with the drums

Loop a short passage and listen to the relationship between bass and kick. They do not need to play every note together. Check where they meet, where one leaves room for the other and where the bass leads into a chord change.

Slow practice playback if needed, then return to the written speed. Keep a fill together with its following downbeat so you practise the transition as well as the run itself.

## Keep the bass part useful

Save the arrangement in your library or export a native project file. A text tab is useful for reading the fret positions. MIDI carries the pitches and timing into a DAW; MusicXML supports a notation workflow in another editor.
